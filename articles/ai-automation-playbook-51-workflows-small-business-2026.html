What "ready-to-run" means here

Each workflow is a concrete trigger → action pair you can stand up in an afternoon:

  • Inbound triage — new lead form → CRM row → Slack ping → draft reply.
  • Invoice chase — overdue invoice → polite reminder → escalate after N days.
  • Support deflection — incoming ticket → knowledge-base answer → human only if unresolved.
  • Content repurposing — one post → thread + newsletter section + social caption.

The stack that keeps it free to run

You do not need a six-figure automation platform. Two open-source tools cover most of it:

  • n8n — visual workflow builder with a large integration marketplace (watch its fair-code license for commercial use).
  • Automatisch — MIT-licensed, free to self-host commercially, no per-seat ceiling.

Both run on your own box. The difference that matters for a business is the license — see Self-Hosted Automation Compared for the exact clauses.

How to pick your first three workflows

1. List the tasks you did manually last week.

2. Mark the ones that are repetitive and low-judgment.

3. Automate those first — highest ROI, lowest risk.

That is the whole method. The playbook just hands you 51 vetted starting points so you skip the blank-page phase.
