When building AI agents that need to solve complex logistics, scheduling, or allocation problems, you're essentially asking: how do I find the best combination of resources under constraints? This is combinatorial optimization — and it's where many practitioners turn to either freelance experts or commercial tools.

Let's look at a practical example. You're building an agent that schedules 100 tasks across 15 workers, with each task having different durations and dependencies. Your goal is to minimize total completion time while respecting worker availability and task precedence.

The Freelance Route

Hiring a freelance operations research expert can be tempting — especially when you need something custom built. However, it's costly and slow:

  • A mid-level OR consultant charges $100–200/hour
  • For your 100-task, 15-worker problem, expect 20–40 hours of work
  • Total cost: $2,000–8,000
  • You're also dependent on their availability and understanding

But there's a better way.

The Paid Alternative: Operations Optimization Pack

Enter the Operations Optimization Pack for AI Agents — a private, build-once solution that embeds combinatorial optimization math directly into your agent workflows. It’s not a black box. You can run it with Python, and it handles complex constraints out of the box.

Here's how to use it in practice:

```python

from operations_optimization import Scheduler

Define tasks: (task_id, duration, dependencies)

tasks = [

("A", 5, []),

("B", 3, ["A"]),

("C", 4, ["A"]),

("D", 2, ["B", "C"])

Define workers

workers = ["W1", "W2", "W3"]
```
